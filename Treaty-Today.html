Land and Geography

Treaty 1 covers rivers, farmland, and urban areas in southern Manitoba. This land has supported hunting, fishing, travel, and cultural traditions for Indigenous peoples for generations.

Government Relationships

Treaty 1 continues to guide how governments and First Nations work together. It influences land use, infrastructure, legal agreements, and community development.

Cultural Identity

Treaty 1 supports the protection of Indigenous languages, traditions, and cultural identity through education programs and community initiatives.

Treaty 1 in Everyday Life

Many people live and work on Treaty 1 land without realizing it. Treaty agreements still affect city planning, public services, and how communities are structured today.

Urban Reserves & Economic Growth

Urban reserves such as Kapyong and businesses developed by Long Plain First Nation create jobs, support economic growth, and strengthen Indigenous governance.

Reconciliation

Reconciliation involves improving relationships between Indigenous and non-Indigenous peoples through education, respect, and understanding of Treaties.

Why Should We Care About Treaty 1?

Treaty 1 affects land use, government decisions, and relationships in Manitoba today.

Understanding Treaties helps recognize the shared history between Indigenous Nations and Canada.

Learning about Treaty 1 also supports reconciliation by including Indigenous perspectives in education and society.

Treaties are still active today and continue to influence laws, communities, and development across Canada.
